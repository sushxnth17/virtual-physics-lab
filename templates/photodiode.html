Experiment: 6

PHOTODIODE CHARACTERISTICS

AIM

To obtain reverse bias characteristics of the given photodiode and hence determine its photo responsivity.

APPARATUS

Photodiode, Light Emitting Diode (LED), DC regulated power supply, Ammeter, Voltmeter and connecting wires.

INTRODUCTION

A photodiode is a semiconductor diode (p-n junction) which converts light energy into electrical current. When light of sufficient energy is incident on the diode, it generates an electron-hole pair. These carriers are separated by the electric field in the depletion region, producing photocurrent.

When the diode is reverse biased, the depletion region widens, increasing the efficiency of light absorption and charge separation.

FORMULA

1. Resistance: R = V / I = 1 / slope

2. Photo Responsivity: Responsivity = Photocurrent / Light Intensity

CIRCUIT DIAGRAM

Figure: Circuit diagram for photodiode characteristics experiment.

NATURE OF GRAPH

  • Reverse bias characteristic of photodiode (I vs V)
  • Variation of photocurrent with light intensity

PROCEDURE

  1. Connect the circuit as shown in the diagram.
  2. Keep the light source at a fixed distance from the photodiode.
  3. Vary the reverse bias voltage from 0 to 5V.
  4. Note the current for each voltage.
  5. Plot graph of current vs voltage.
  6. Fix voltage and vary light intensity.
  7. Note current for different intensities.
  8. Plot graph of current vs intensity.
  9. Determine slope to find responsivity.

OBSERVATION

  • The photodiode exhibits a linear relationship between applied voltage and reverse current, confirming semiconductor diode characteristics.
  • As voltage increases in reverse bias, the depletion region widens, allowing more carriers to be collected.
  • Photo responsivity varies linearly with light intensity, demonstrating the photoelectric effect in the semiconductor junction.
  • The slope of the I vs V graph represents the dynamic resistance (dI/dV), which determines device responsivity.
  • The inverse of the I vs V slope equals the device resistance at the operating point.
