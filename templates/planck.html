Experiment: 9

DETERMINATION OF PLANCK'S CONSTANT

AIM

To determine the Planck's constant using known LEDs.

APPARATUS

Variable power supply (0 - 10V), DC voltmeter, Different LEDs

INTRODUCTION

Max Planck's quantum theory and Albert Einstein's theory of relativity have forced man to change some of his most cherished philosophical beliefs. Max Planck in his paper published in 1900 announced his derivations based on his revolutionary idea that the energy emitted by a resonator (black body radiator) is in discrete values or in quanta.

The packet of energy is given by

E = hν = hc / λ

Where

c is the velocity of light

λ is the wavelength of the light emitted

h is Planck's constant

Light Emitting Diode (LED)

LED is a two terminal solid-state lamp, which emits light with very low voltage and current. If V is the forward voltage applied across the LED terminals that makes it emit light then the energy given to the LED is given by

E = eV

Where e is electronic charge

LEDs are very high efficiency diodes and hence this entire electrical energy is converted into light energy, then equating the above equations,

eV = hc / λ

From which Planck's constant is given by

h = eVλ / c

LED knee voltage

Knee voltage of diode is the potential applied across the pn junction, that is self-sufficient to overcome barrier potential and a constant current flow is maintained through the diode.

PROCEDURE

  1. The circuit is rigged as shown in the circuit diagram.
  2. The knee voltage is measured for given color LED light.
  3. The trial is repeated for various LEDs.
  4. Multiply the knee voltage with crest factor to get the corrected knee voltage (V).
  5. Plot corrected knee voltage V vs 1/λ and calculate the slope.
  6. Planck's constant is calculated using the given formula.
